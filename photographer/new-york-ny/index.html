Where in New York

You set the spot, so a request can start anywhere in the five boroughs. In Manhattan that means Midtown, the Financial District, SoHo, the West Village, Chelsea, the Lower East Side, the Upper West Side, the Upper East Side, Harlem, and Washington Heights. In Brooklyn, DUMBO, Brooklyn Heights, Williamsburg, Greenpoint, Park Slope, Bushwick, Bed-Stuy, and out to Coney Island. In Queens, Long Island City, Astoria, Jackson Heights, and Flushing. Plus the Bronx and Staten Island.

New York spots that photograph well

  • Central ParkBethesda Terrace, Bow Bridge, the elm arch over the Mall, and Gapstow Bridge with the skyline behind it. Four completely different pictures inside one park, which is why a longer session pays off here more than anywhere else in the city.
  • Washington Street, DUMBOThe Manhattan Bridge framed between the warehouses. It is also a live street with cars on it and a line of people waiting their turn, so this is a five minute stop rather than a session.
  • Brooklyn Bridge ParkThe whole downtown skyline across the water, the carousel, and the piers. Sunset lands on Manhattan from here, which is the right way round.
  • Brooklyn Heights PromenadeSame view, higher up, quieter, and with a rail to lean on. Better with kids than the waterfront.
  • The High LinePlantings, steel, and the city pressed in on both sides. Narrow and busy, so early morning is the version worth having.
  • Little IslandConcrete tulips over the Hudson with the sunset behind. Small, timed, and worth checking entry rules before you build a plan on it.
  • Gantry Plaza State Park, Long Island CityThe Midtown skyline straight across the East River, the old gantries, and far fewer people than the Brooklyn side.
  • Washington Square ParkThe arch, the fountain, and NYU all around it. The most reliably alive backdrop in the city on a spring afternoon.
  • Grand Central TerminalThe main concourse ceiling and the light through the windows. Handheld only, and the building has its own rules about anything more than that.
  • Fort Tryon ParkThe Hudson, the Palisades, and the Cloisters. Uptown, uncrowded, and the best fall color on the island.
  • BushwickBlocks of painted walls that change every year. If you want a picture that could not be from anywhere else, this is it.
  • Coney IslandThe boardwalk, the Wonder Wheel, and the beach. Off-season it is empty and strange and photographs beautifully.
  • The Staten Island FerryFree, runs all night, and gives you the harbor and the statue from the deck. Twenty minutes each way is exactly one session.

The practical note, and it matters more here than anywhere: New York has real rules about photography in public space. They generally turn on equipment, crew and setup rather than on a person taking pictures, but Central Park, the High Line, the observation decks and the transit system each publish their own policy, and some spots are restricted regardless. Check the specific place before you plan around it.

What people shoot in New York

Proposals

This is the proposal capital and everybody already knows the spots: Bow Bridge, the Brooklyn Bridge Park piers, Gapstow with the skyline behind it, the Hudson River rail at sunset. The advantage of an on-demand photographer is that nobody has to loiter with a long lens for twenty minutes first, which is what usually gives it away.

Visiting for a few days

You are standing somewhere famous with your whole group, and the choice is a selfie stick or handing a $1,200 phone to a stranger. Neither is good. A five or ten minute session at the spot you are already standing at is the entire pitch.

Content days

Creators shoot here because six blocks give you six looks. SoHo cast iron, a Chelsea corner, a Bushwick wall, the LIC waterfront. Book an hour and cover a neighborhood.

Headshots

Fifteen minutes outside your building beats a half day in a studio for a photo that is going on LinkedIn and nowhere else. North-facing shade on a side street is the whole trick.

Graduation

NYU, Columbia, Fordham, CUNY, and every high school in the boroughs. May is the crunch, campus rules vary, and the gates plus the nearest park usually get it done.

December

Rockefeller Center, Bryant Park, the windows on Fifth. The sun is gone before five, so the whole evening is usable, and the city does the lighting for you.

Birthdays and everything ordinary

The stoop, the block, the corner you have lived on for nine years. Some of the best photos of New York are not of the landmarks at all.

New York questions

Can I book an OfMe photographer in NYC today?

Not yet. OfMe is coming to the App Store and New York City is one of the first areas we are opening. We are not publishing a launch date until we can hold it.

Which parts of the city can I request from?

You set the pin, so anywhere in the five boroughs. Whether a photographer accepts depends on who is available near that pin, and here that means who can reach you on foot or by train quickly rather than who is closest as the crow flies.

Do I need a permit in Central Park or on the High Line?

The rules generally turn on equipment, crew and setup rather than on a person taking pictures, but each of those places publishes its own policy and some spots are restricted regardless. Check the specific place first.

How much does a session cost?

From $9.99 for 5 minutes to $64.99 for a full hour, the same as every other OfMe area. That is the standard price list. Requesting one specific photographer by name costs 1.5 times the list price, and extending at the end of your session is priced at 10 percent off the extra time.

I am visiting for three days. Is this useful?

It is one of the clearest cases for it. Nobody in your group wants to be the one holding the phone, and asking a passerby gets you a blurry frame with a head cut off.

Can I book at night?

There is no cutoff time in the app, and night here is often better than day. In December the sun is down before five, which makes the whole evening usable.

Does the photographer keep my photos?

No. Photos never reach their camera roll, and their access is revoked the moment your session is delivered. See Support for how delivery, refunds, and reporting work.
