Where in the county

You set the spot, so a request can start anywhere from the Martin County line to the Broward line. The names people use are West Palm Beach, Palm Beach, Palm Beach Gardens, North Palm Beach, Jupiter, Juno Beach, Tequesta, Riviera Beach, Singer Island, Lake Worth Beach, Lantana, Boynton Beach, Delray Beach, Boca Raton, Wellington, Royal Palm Beach, Greenacres, and Loxahatchee.

Spots that photograph well

  • Worth Avenue and the vias, Palm BeachArched passages, tiled courtyards, and bougainvillea. The vias are small, which is perfect for a five or ten minute session and wrong for a group of twelve.
  • The Lake Trail, Palm BeachPaved path along the Intracoastal with the water on one side and the hedges on the other. Quiet in the morning and easy to walk with a stroller.
  • The West Palm waterfront on Flagler DrivePalms, the docks, and the island across the water. Late afternoon light comes back across the Intracoastal and lands right.
  • Clematis Street and Rosemary SquareDowntown West Palm without the water. Color, storefronts, and shade, which matters here more than it sounds.
  • Jupiter Inlet Lighthouse and Dubois ParkThe red lighthouse over the inlet is the most recognizable thing in the north county. The park below it has calm water and shade for kids.
  • Juno Beach PierLong pier, wide beach, and sunrise straight out of the ocean. One of the easiest good photos in the county.
  • Atlantic Avenue and the Delray beachAwnings and palms on the avenue, then two blocks east you are on the sand. A twenty minute session covers both.
  • Mizner Park, Boca RatonThe plaza, the fountains, and the arcades. Evening here is genuinely pleasant even in August.
  • Red Reef Park and the Boca beachesSea grape and dune walkovers. The walkovers frame a shot better than the open sand does.
  • WellingtonHorses, hedges, and white fence. During the winter equestrian season this is the most distinctive backdrop in the county, and it is on private show grounds, so ask about access first.
  • Peanut IslandWater on every side and the Singer Island skyline. You need a boat or the shuttle, so plan the longer session.
  • Okeeheelee and the western parksLakes, pines, and space. Where you go when you want green rather than turquoise.

The practical note: rules here are set town by town, not countywide, and several municipalities treat a paid photo session differently from a family with a phone. Public beach access points and public parks are usually fine for a short handheld session. Private clubs, resorts, ticketed gardens, and the equestrian grounds are not public and set their own rules. Check the specific place first.

What people shoot in Palm Beach County

Family in town for the season

Between November and April this county fills up with people visiting parents and grandparents. Three generations in the same place for four days is the single best reason to book twenty minutes on a beach walkover before everybody flies home.

Vacation photos

You are on a beach, the light is doing all the work, and the only thing missing is somebody to hold the camera. That is a five minute problem with a nine dollar answer.

Engagements and proposals

The Lake Trail, the Jupiter inlet, or the end of a dune walkover at sunrise. Sunrise is underused here and the beaches are empty at that hour.

Personal branding and headshots

A lot of people run a business out of this county and need a current photo that is not from 2019. Ten minutes outside your building in West Palm or Boca is the whole job.

Equestrian

Wellington in the winter season is its own photo economy. OfMe sessions are short and mobile, which suits a barn aisle or a ring rail better than a full booked shoot, subject to the venue saying yes.

Holiday cards in seventy-eight degrees

December here is the good month. Palms, bare feet, and the card that annoys everybody back north. Book by early December, because the week before Christmas is the busiest week of the year for photos everywhere.

Dogs

Early morning on the sand, or the shaded loops in the western parks. Dog rules differ by beach and by season, so check the town before you build a plan on it.

Palm Beach County questions

Can I book an OfMe photographer here today?

Not yet. OfMe is coming to the App Store and Palm Beach County is one of the first areas we are opening. We are not publishing a launch date until we can hold it.

Which parts of the county can I request from?

You set the pin, so anywhere from Tequesta down to Boca and west to Wellington and Loxahatchee. Whether a photographer accepts depends on who is available near that pin at that moment, which is the honest answer for any on-demand service.

What happens if no photographer accepts?

The request expires on its own, you are not charged, and you can try again later. Your card is only authorized at booking, never charged for a session that never happened, and that authorization releases on its own.

How much does a session cost?

From $9.99 for 5 minutes to $64.99 for a full hour, the same as every other OfMe area. That is the standard price list. Requesting one specific photographer by name costs 1.5 times the list price, and extending at the end of your session is priced at 10 percent off the extra time.

Do I need a permit on the beach?

It is set town by town and park by park rather than countywide, and some places treat a paid session differently from a family with a phone. A short handheld session at a public access point is usually fine. Check the specific town, and assume private clubs and resorts have their own rules.

When should I book, time of day and time of year?

Sunrise over the ocean, since the whole coast faces east, or the last hour before sunset over the Intracoastal. November through April is the comfortable half of the year. June through September, go early and watch the afternoon storms.

Does the photographer keep my photos?

No. Photos never reach their camera roll, and their access is revoked the moment your session is delivered. See Support for how delivery, refunds, and reporting work.
